# Reflection on Professionalism at Focus Bear

## Have you ever experienced or witnessed great teamwork and professionalism? What made it effective?

Yes, working in a student team I noticed that we collaborated well when we were all engaged and responsive. In the team, the different personalities played to different strengths, and I think we were all respectful of each other, understanding when we needed each other's help and different learning styles. We also always were respectful and never demeaning when people asked questions, and I do believe we always assumed good intent in messages and emails.

I also believe the team I was in was professional. We made agendas for meetings with supervisors and clients, sending them for reference prior to meetings, and meeting to make the agendas to ensure we were asking pertinent and thorough questions. During these meetings, everyone was engaged, and those who were not presenting took notes, that is, everyone made sure they were contributing in some way.

When addressing our supervisor and our client, we also made sure to be direct without lacking respect or politeness, and we had created policies for actions like sending emails, to ensure we were consistent and professional in our communications.

## What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?

Adhering to the communication channels outlined by Focus Bear, I can make sure that I am using the correct platform for the context (i.e. Discord for administrative questions that need a response quickly, emails for responses within 48 hours). In those communications, I should also make sure my tone is respectful, and the communication is direct and easy to understand.

Be honest and take the time to get to know the team I am working with, so I can ensure people I am working with are comfortable around me. Also take time to learn correct pronouns, and preferences in communicating.

Understand what I am trying to say before speaking or writing, and taking time to make sure that my main point is clear, along with the purpose of communication.

I believe also not making assumptions from people's preferred communication styles is important, and in regular contexts avoid assumptions. Accessibility-wise, if I am hosting the meeting, I could take measures to make sure the meeting works for everyone, that is, turn on closed captions or add AI meeting notes if everyone consents. I know this from my own preference, but asking questions and giving enough pause for people to process thoughts and respond is also very important for people feeling comfortable.

I should also use inclusive language, and identity-first language (Creating Accessible and Inclusive Communications, 2024).

## How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?

Ensure my communications are respectful, polite, and happen during appropriate work hours through the vetted channels. Also raise any behaviour that is not safe to the allocated Focus Bear team, to maintain a safe and respectful environment.

I also think being authentic, open in my communications (but still professional) is the best way to create a positive work environment, as it creates opportunities for trust between myself and people I will work with.

Showing gratitude for help I believe is instrumental in making the workplace fun and fulfilling, even if it is a small task, it always is a positive impact when you can express gratitude to someone for helping you.

## How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?

Ensure that I have taken steps to answer my own questions, before asking them to avoid asking questions that are redundant or documented already. If I have a direction in mind when completing a task, I should present reasons why I want to take that direction so I can ask before immediately doing said tasks.

Preparing an agenda would assist to ensure I am not wasting colleagues and supervisors time and that I am clear in my communications. I should have any necessary documents or windows open in case I need to screen share, and sending the agenda prior to the meeting I find is genuinely good practice if it is a longer meeting.

Careful proof reading of questions I have and my work (if it is the topic of the meeting) I think would also help me prepare so I am not presenting something with easily resolvable mistakes or without care.

Arriving early to meetings is also important, to ensure my supervisor and colleagues are never waiting for me.

## What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?

Focus Bear's internship structure seems to provide a lot of opportunities to be proactive. I think there are several avenues I could pursue to be proactive:

- While working through onboarding, I could submit any issues I find with the application, logging them in detail with recreation steps.
- Check all the provided information before raising a question, and try to first resolve it myself if it simple with little dependencies.
- From above, still being careful with researching before asking questions, but asking questions as soon as possible once I have done the appropriate research, so I am not idle waiting on a response and creating blockers for myself.
- While completing onboarding, do general tasks to earn internship points to contribute to the Focus Bear team. For example, write a new standard operating procedure.
- Attend all meetings invited to, as often as possible, listening actively and engaging in the meeting.
- Get to know people in the team!
- Try to use Focus Bear as often as possible, to gain familiarity with the application's features.

## How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information/action from them? How would you escalate it further if you don't get a response and when would you escalate?

Depending on the type of issue, I would typically send a polite follow email that restates the purpose of the communication - I know it is easy for communications to get buried. If it is urgent, I would message in Discord in the correct channel, tagging the person.

If there was no response for two business days or so, I would follow up again in the same format, switching platforms if it increases in urgency. After another 2 days, I would escalate to get help, so I would message another member of the team that could put me in touch with the colleague or supervisor, respectfully explaining why I need the information or action from the individual.

If the issue was urgent and impacting myself and other's critical work, I would immediately escalate by messaging another person in the team, or bringing it up in my stand-up (if it is team-related).

## What are some things you won't do?

- Violate Focus Bear's policies
- Speak negatively about coworkers
- Share private information about Focus Bear or individuals working at Focus Bear
- Be dishonest about any of my work
- Be disrespectful
- Complete other tasks unrelated to my Focus Bear internship during working hours (Mondays, Wednesdays, Fridays)
- Miss meetings without prior notice unless an unforeseen emergency
