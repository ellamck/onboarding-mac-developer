# Cyber Security Guidelines

## Research & Learn

**What are common cyber security threats in a remote work environment?**

According to SentinelOne (2024):

- Phishing attacks
- Weak passwords
- Using unauthorised applications or services by employees (Shadow IT)
- Insecure wifi
- Unpatched software, especially if it is Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)
- Malware and ransomware
- Lack of multi-factor authentication

**What are best practices for keeping your devices and accounts secure?**

Also according to SentinelOne (2024), these are some of the best practices to keep devices and accounts secure:

- Use only secure wifi networks
- Use strong passwords and update them regularly. Don't reuse the same password
- Use multi-factor authentication when you can
- Use only company-approved tools for work

**Why is it important to lock your computer when away from your desk?**

It prevents unauthorised access to your device, and it is a simple way to protect sensitive information and the integrity of your device.

**How should you handle phishing attempts and suspicious links?**

Do not click on the link, and if it was on email, forward the email to the relevant reporting party. If it was through a platform, report the attempt to the platform (Australian Government, 2023). For SMS, report the attempt to your provider (Australian Government, 2023).

**What makes a strong password, and why should you use a password manager?**

Qualities of a strong password (CISA, n.d.):

- Length: ideally 16 characters or more
- Random
- Unique

Password managers help you create strong and unique passwords, and store them securely (“Password Managers | Cyber.Gov.Au,” 2024).

**Why is two-factor authentication (2FA) important, and when should you enable it?**

It is significantly more powerful against attacks, as it requires another proof of identity to access your account (Australian Cyber Security Centre, n.d.). If an option, you should enable it as soon as possible.

## Reflection

**What security measures do you currently follow, and where can you improve?**

I have a password manager, and never repeat passwords. My password manager also notifies me for when I should switch passwords, so I update them regularly as well. I also use MFA often, whenever I can. Through my student club, I attended a "Protect Yourself" workshop where I also implemented further security measures on my devices, such as turning off the setting where if I close my MacBook and immediately reopen within a minute or two, it doesn't require a password - I turned that off to ensure my laptop locks whenever I close it.

What I could improve upon is updating my software more timely. I often postpone it, like updating my Google Chrome, when I should be updating as soon as I am alerted there is a new update.

**How can you make secure behaviour a habit rather than an afterthought?**

Stay up to date with device protections, and learn what are good security habits. This way, I will be more likely to implement the habits if I understand why they are important!

**What steps will you take to ensure your passwords and accounts are secure?**

Update my passwords regularly, and only use unique strong passwords. Pay attention to suspicious behaviour, and use MFA whenever available.

**What would you do if you suspected a security breach or suspicious activity on your account?**

Suspend my accounts, and notify the relevant authorities (for example, if it is Focus Bear, I would reach out to the team or the privacy email to alert them).
