# Inclusive Design Reflection

## Research & Learn

**Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?**

According to the Australian government (Australian Government, 2022), vulnerable populations can include:

- Children
- Seniors
- People with impaired intellectual functioning
- People with impaired physical functioning
- People who are Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islanders
- People who are not native speakers of the local language
- People with low levels of literacy or education
- People subject to human exploitation and control (human trafficking, child labour)

Also, according to New South Wales' government website, neurodivergent people are also considered to be a vulnerable population ("Neurodivergent people", 2025).

**What ethical considerations are important when designing or working with neurodivergent individuals? (Hint: Avoid overwhelming UX, respect sensory needs, ensure clear communication.)**

### Ethical Design Considerations

Please note, all of this information is gathered from “The Principles of Neurodivergent UX Design Every Designer Should Know,” 2026.

For people with ADHD, the UX design should minimise distractions and be clear, and have distinct breakdowns of actions. It shouldn't present everything at once, and it should be easy to go backwards a step or edit fields, with clear error messaging (“The Principles of Neurodivergent UX Design Every Designer Should Know,” 2026).

It is important to consider font, line spacing, length, and alignment of text for Dyslexia as well. Clearer letterforms and emphasis makes the interface accessible for people with Dyslexia. AccessibilityChecker states the following for typography settings:

- Sans-serif fonts or specialised fonts like OpenDyslexic
- Line heights of at least 1.5
- Line lengths of maximum 70-80 characters
- Left-aligned body text
- Bold used for emphasis

For people on the Austism spectrum, it is important to have clear language and layouts, and sensory input that is not distressing. Buttons should be descriptive so users know what will happen if they press it, in fact, all actions should clearly describe the outcomes of the action from the button. Colour palettes that are soft and easy to look at, along with dark modes should be used in the design. UX animations should likely be kept to a minimum or always used thoughtfully, "as auto-playing animations, looping videos, and sudden transitions can cause real sensory distress" (“The Principles of Neurodivergent UX Design Every Designer Should Know,” 2026).

Within design, the text and content should be clear and not employ idioms, metaphors or sarcasm so that the intent is clear, and so as much unpredictability (and the distress from it) as possible is removed. The same occurs with navigation, consistent positioning and layout in design helps the user free their cognitive energy for what they actually want to do, not on orienting themselves.

### Ethical Communication Considerations

According to New South Wales' (NSW) Government, it is important to understand that people communicate differently and can require more time to process information or form their thoughts. Simple and clear communication should be used to be accessible, and it is considerate to discuss the best mode of communication and engagement for interacting with the person with neurodivergence.

It is good to also enquire to understand what measures help the person feel and be more comfortable, along with preparing tools that help with meetings. The government of NSW recommends using tools that support the needs for effective engagement, and that engagement is structured and consistent.

**How can you make interactions and content more accessible for people with ADHD or Autism? (e.g., simple language, predictable navigation, reducing cognitive load)**

As outlined above, the following can help interactions and content become more accessible for people with ADHD and Autism:

1. Clear communication that does not include hard to interpret language (jargon, metaphors etc.), with a direct intention
2. Reduce cognitive load through descriptive buttons and consistent navigation and layouts
3. Reduce animations/motion on the screen that is overstimulating and/or distressing (colours as well)
4. Reduce distractions, and break down actions or long form content into clear items
5. Use Dyslexia-friendly fonts and typography choices

**How can we support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting? (Hint: Be clear in communication, respect different working styles, and allow flexibility.)**

When communicating, be clear and direct in speech, and perhaps put important takeaways or information discussed in writing, to allow for team members to process the information. Understand team member's different needs, and be flexible in meetings, and understanding of different communication and work styles.

Asking for preferences could be very helpful, that is, asking if they prefer written communication over verbal, etc.

## Reflection

**How can you adjust your communication style to be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates?**

I can avoid using hard to interpret language, such as metaphors, idioms, etc. and ensure I am being clear and direct when communicating. I can also perform the communication in the format the user or teammate prefers, to ensure they are fully comfortable. When sending documents or emails, I can also make sure that I use the formats I have outlined above (Dyslexia-friendly font, left aligned text, broken down paragraphs) so I am communicating in a way that is accessible to everyone.

**What are some common UX or communication pitfalls that might make Focus Bear less accessible or supportive?**

- Navigation that is not consistent: the user needs to reorient themselves each page and it is hard to keep track of how to navigate the interface
- Unclear button descriptions: hard to tell what an action button does, creating uncertainty in the user
- Lack of options to turn off UX features: not being able to turn off notification sounds, adding sensory input for the user that is unwanted

**What is one practical change you can make in your work to better support vulnerable populations?**

Ensure my documentation, emails, content, etc. uses the formats outlined in my research section about ethical considerations. Consistently use direct language, and be considerate of different working styles and communication formats.

Thank you for writing, I am sorry to hear you are struggling with getting started on tasks. Many people, including myself, find this challenging, and it is not a reflection of your motivation and capability.
