# Kanban Workflow

## Research & Learn

**How does a Kanban board work, and how does it help manage workflow?**

Each task is a card on the board, and there are different work stages for the tasks to move through. Kanban manages workflow by limiting the amount of work in progress tasks, and moving how tasks move from not started to completed (Radigan, 2024).

**What do the different columns on a Kanban board represent? (e.g., Backlog, In Progress, Blocked, Done)**

The different columns are work stages for tasks.

Backlog: These are tasks that have been designed for the product, but they have not started being developed or worked on yet.

In progress: Task being actively worked on.

Blocked: The task cannot be completed due to other dependencies from other tasks, or another blocker (waiting on communication from an external source, etc.)

Done: The task is completed, with the appropriate definition of done.

**How do tasks move through the board, and who is responsible for updating them?**

The tasks move to match their development, that is, if a task has been claimed by someone and they've started on it, they would move the task from Backlog to In progress. The team member working on the task is responsible for moving the task on the board, however it is up to the team if they want to allocate a specific role for updating tasks.

**What are the benefits of limiting work in progress (WIP)?**

This ensures the team is not spread too thin working on too many tasks at once. Limiting the WIP helps keep work flowing and the team focused. It also reduces context switching (Radigan, 2024).
