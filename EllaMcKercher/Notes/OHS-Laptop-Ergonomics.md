# Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) for Desk-Based Work

## Research and Learn

**What are the risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard?**

#### Keyboard

When working without an external keyboard, tendonitis can occur, which is the inflammation of tendons and can occur from the elbow downwards, especially in the hand (Princeton University, 2019). This happens because the wrists are resting at a downward angle from your fingers, creating an unnatural angle. General aches and strains may also occur if the keyboard being used is not ergonomic and forces your hands, forearms and wrists at unnatural angles for long periods of time (Princeton University, 2019).

#### Monitor

As with the keyboard, a lot of the possible risks from not using an external monitor arise from bad posture. If the monitor is not at the proper height and distance away, sitting at your desk can cause muscle strain (including eye strain) and back pain because of the way you need to hold your body to properly see the screen (Princeton University, 2019).

**What ergonomic equipment can improve posture when working on a laptop? (e.g., laptop stands, external keyboards, ergonomic chairs)**

Ergonomic Chairs

- Back of the chair conforms to the user's back
- Padded seat and there should be no arms

Desks

- A sit-stand desk allows the user to alternate between sitting and standing which helps avoid sitting or standing for prolonged periods

Laptop Stand

- So that the laptop screen can be at eye level - it is beneficial to use an external keyboard and mouse if possible

Mouse

- Ergonomic mouse to relax the fingers and take off strain from the hands

All of these recommendations I found from the University of New South Wales (“Ergonomic Equipment - Safety | Assurance and Integrity,” 2018).

**What adjustments should be made to monitor height, chair position, and desk setup for a healthier workspace?**
Monitors should be slightly below eye level and straight in front of you, at an arm's length away from your face (Princeton University, 2019). If needing to use a laptop, the keys G and H should be in line with your navel (“Ergonomic Equipment - Safety | Assurance and Integrity,” 2018).

Chair height should allow for your elbows to be bent at 90 degrees, and on your desk, your fingers should be at the same level as your forearm (Princeton University, 2019). Your thighs should be parallel to the floor as well, and the back of the chair should support you enough so that you can sit in an upright position with a neutral spine (Princeton University, 2019).

For sit-stand desks in stand position, ensure your elbows are resting at a 90 degree angle, with the monitor just below eye level. The monitor should be an arms length away (“Ergonomic Equipment - Safety | Assurance and Integrity,” 2018).

**What are some daily habits that reduce the impact of prolonged laptop use? (e.g., movement breaks, posture checks, eye strain reduction techniques)**
Regular movement breaks, about every ten minutes, with getting up to walk and stretch every hour. Princeton's Health Services also recommends to "vary your motions by changing tasks" (Princeton University, 2019). Periods of sitting or standing should be broken up with micro-breaks, which are 1-2 minute breaks (“Ergonomic Equipment - Safety | Assurance and Integrity,” 2018).

For eye strain, dedicated computer glasses can help if necessary, and every 20 minutes, look at something 6 metres away for 20 seconds ("Good posture and eye health at work | Eyes On Optometrists," 2025).

Regular posture checks help both with mitigating eye strain and muscle fatigue ( (“10 Ways to Address Eye Fatigue Caused by Displays | EIZO,” 2025).
